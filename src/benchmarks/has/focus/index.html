    What is being tested?

    The DOM fixture mounts two sibling subtrees of 500 nested items each (#outer and #target), plus a direct-child <a href="#"> inside #target. Each test toggles focus() and blur() on an <a> element and flushes styles via getComputedStyle($target).outlineColor across two focus locations:

    • Direct Child Focus: Focusing #target > a (matched by all 6 selectors).
    • Deep Descendant Focus: Focusing a deeply nested <a> inside #target (matched by the 4 descendant selectors).

    1. Bare :focus vs. a:focus

    • Identical invalidation when focusing <a>: Because the focused element is an <a>, it matches both :focus and a:focus. Toggling focus walks the same ancestor chain and marks the same :has() anchor dirty.
    • Subtree re-evaluation still dominates: Qualifying :focus with a tag name (a:focus) performs nearly identically to bare :focus; scoping the subject (#target vs. :root) and using the child combinator (>) are what reduce subtree walks.

    2. :root vs. #target Prefix

    • Smaller subtree scan on blur(): When focus is blurred inside #target, #target:has(:focus) only walks #target’s subtree (500 items) to verify no other descendant is focused, whereas :root:has(:focus) must scan the entire document (both #outer and #target, 1,000 items total).

    3. Child Combinator (>) vs. Descendant

    • Shallow re-evaluation: When focusing and blurring a direct child of #target, #target:has(> :focus) and #target:has(> a:focus) only inspect direct children of #target rather than traversing all 500 nested subtrees (~1.7–1.8× faster than #target:has(:focus) and ~2× faster than :root:has(:focus)).
